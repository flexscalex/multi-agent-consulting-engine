# Deliverable rubric

The partner writes against this. The QC editor checks against it. A deliverable that fails a rule marked MATERIAL goes back to the partner. Rules marked STYLE are fixed by the partner but do not block delivery on their own.

## Evidence

1. MATERIAL. Every factual statement in the memo and report cites a fact ID from FACT_BASE.md, or is listed in ASSUMPTIONS_REGISTER.md and labeled as an assumption where it appears.
2. MATERIAL. Every number carries a tag: measured (with its source ID), derived (with the inputs and method, or the ID of a derived fact), or assumed. A range is a number.
3. MATERIAL. No fact ID is cited for a claim its text does not support. QC spot-checks at least ten.
4. MATERIAL. A claim whose fact is marked contradicted, not_reproduced, or unreachable in the ledger is not presented as established. It may appear only with that status stated.
5. MATERIAL. Nothing from QUARANTINE.md appears as a conclusion unless the report shows the fact IDs that now support it. The prior team's opinions are discussed only in the section "What the prior team believed, and what the facts say now".
6. MATERIAL. Where the client's questions are unanswered, the report says so and shows the decision under each plausible answer. It does not pick one silently.

## Decision quality

7. MATERIAL. The options considered are listed before the recommendation, with the criteria used to compare them and how each option scores. "Do nothing" and "do less" are always among the options.
8. MATERIAL. There is one verdict from: proceed, proceed with conditions, do not proceed, insufficient evidence. Conditions are specific and testable.
9. MATERIAL. Every risk has a severity (fatal, material, minor) and either a mitigation with a fact ID or the statement that no mitigation is known.
10. MATERIAL. The report states what would flip the verdict. At least one item is a fact that could realistically be found in the next 90 days.
11. MATERIAL. Where lenses disagreed, the report says who disagreed, why, and how the partner resolved it. Agreement among lenses is never itself cited as evidence.
12. MATERIAL. Every red-team challenge rated material is answered: resolved with evidence, or carried as open with its resolution route.

## Confidence

13. MATERIAL. Each major section ends with a confidence level (high, medium, low) and one sentence on what it rests on. High requires verified facts only.
14. MATERIAL. PRIMARY_RESEARCH_LIST.md is ranked, and each item says what answer would change the verdict. No item is vague ("talk to experts").

## Form

15. STYLE. The decision memo fits on one page: verdict, recommendation in two sentences, three reasons, three risks, what flips it, five validation steps with who to call, confidence.
16. STYLE. No em dashes anywhere. Use periods, commas, or parentheses.
17. STYLE. Plain language. Short sentences. No consulting filler ("leverage", "synergy", "best-in-class", "world-class"). No selling; there is no section on why this team is the right partner.
18. STYLE. Dates are absolute (2026-09-17), never "recently" or "last year".
19. STYLE. The appendix lists every fact ID used, in order, so a reader can audit the report against the ledger.
20. STYLE. Files are named exactly as the workflow specifies, with the V1_ prefix on deliverables. A rewrite within the same run keeps V1.
